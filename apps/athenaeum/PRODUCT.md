# Product

## Register

product

## Users

David is the primary user, working in Athenaeum throughout the day as a personal knowledge and operations workspace. A small number of collaborators may access shared vaults as a secondary audience.

The primary workflow is writing and reviewing the daily note while applying typed Supertags inline. Supporting workflows include Supertag schema administration, knowledge-graph browsing, calendar and meeting review, bookmarks, workouts, sharing, and agent conversations.

## Product Purpose

Athenaeum is an autonomous, strongly typed second brain. It turns daily notes into structured, attributable knowledge without forcing the user out of the writing flow. Daily notes are the home surface and Supertags are the organizing primitive; the rest of the product supports, enriches, or retrieves that core material.

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, calm. The interface should feel like a dependable command center for an expert user: focused during writing, dense when inspecting structured data, and quiet when secondary tools are not in use.

## Anti-references

Do not present Athenaeum as a feature wall, generic SaaS dashboard, or collection of unrelated cards. Avoid equal-weight navigation for every capability, decorative gradients, visual noise, novelty controls, and any Cloudflare branding or copied component-library identity. Chat, graph, calendar, and other tools should orbit the daily-note-plus-Supertags workflow rather than compete with it.

## Design Principles

- Make the daily note the obvious home and the fastest path to useful work.
- Treat Supertags as a writing primitive and knowledge structure, not an admin-only destination.
- Establish one coherent shell and interaction vocabulary across every route.
- Keep secondary tools available without giving them equal visual weight to the core workflow.
- Show state and provenance clearly so autonomous activity feels inspectable and trustworthy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard-first navigation and visible focus states, preserve usable contrast for text and controls, never rely on color alone for state, and provide reduced-motion behavior. Responsive layouts must retain access to the daily note, navigation, and chat on smaller screens.
